# Household readiness, jars, and family parcels

[Design index](readme.md) · proposed full-game layer for Just a few peppers

**The player is helping a household prepare for winter.** Bulk pepper handling does most of the work. This layer connects it to people and to the property through a few uses of the same carry/place controls. It belongs after the small bulk-interaction test succeeds.

## A bounded household project

| Element | Minimum proposed content | Familiar action | Visible result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Returned jars | One carrier already at the gate when the player arrives. | Carry to the clean-jar return rack. | Gate is clearer and the family's jars are back where they belong. |
| Family parcels | Two boxes: **For Aunt** and **Take to the city**. | Transfer compatible finished food in batches, then place each filled box at the gate. | Food has named recipients; two parcels wait for the family. |
| Grandpa's winter supply | Existing half-stocked cellar with reserved bays for today's food. | Place finished carriers into shelf bays. | The previously empty sections fill visibly. |
| Table under the vine | Three movable work props and one folded cloth. | Put props aside, then place the cloth. | A working surface becomes a place to sit together. |

The old refrigerator/tool cupboard and cellar curtain are short reveal props, not additional chore systems. No jar washing, lid matching, parcel delivery trip, hunger, calendar, or new food recipe is required.

These are one finite set of obligations, signposted on a simple household sketch early in the day. Grandpa's “while you're here” remarks reveal their meaning or location; they do not keep increasing the required totals. Optional favors are separate after the ending.

## Returned jars: circulation without a supply-management game

The returned carrier holds an authored group of empty family jars. A label indicates their owner; shapes and worn labels provide visual history. Place the whole carrier onto the return rack in one action. The jars settle into their assigned storage group and the empty carrier can be reused through the existing handling system.

Basic jar preparation is compressed into the family handoff. The processing line already has adequate prepared supplies for the entire finite harvest, so leaving the returned carrier until later cannot halt production. The return is a small household obligation and a useful opportunity on a return trip, not a repeating fuel requirement.

Returning empty glass does not create pepper progress. It changes the location of the existing jar group and completes that household item once. Repeating pickup/placement cannot duplicate the carrier, generate food, or earn repeated completion credit.

## Cellar versus family food

Both parcels accept either of the two compatible pepper products. Do not introduce a mandatory perfect roast, specific jar shape, or unavailable recipe requirement. The difference is who receives the food. The proposed decision is when and where to allocate a batch, not a difficult packing formula.

Starting target: **one small filled box per recipient**, each accepting 12 pepper-equivalent units of finished food. The interface shows the box filling and the remaining space. Exact unit accounting is for balancing; the player uses the same broad carrier transfer as a machine intake. A 48-unit carrier can fill a 12-unit parcel and retain 36 units for another destination. Labels attach automatically when a box is ready; final placement uses a broad gate target.

Show both parcel targets before the player allocates substantial food. Until **Finish the day**, completed food can be retrieved from a shelf or parcel and reassigned with its recipe/state intact. A filled parcel is ready only while it remains filled and at the gate. If the player takes food back out, its readiness updates. No food leaves the map through a simulated visitor before the ending.

For the current illustrative 732-unit harvest, the two parcels use 24 units and Grandpa's shelves receive 708. At the provisional 3:1 conversion that is **8 jar equivalents in parcels plus 236 on shelves**, totaling the existing 244. Parcels are destinations for existing production, not extra production added at the end. If the harvest budget changes, validate the parcel targets and storage capacity together.

The outdoor reserve rack is temporary storage before cellar access. Transferring its food to shelves or parcels removes it from that rack. Food on the temporary rack is not yet fully allocated at the ending. Pre-existing compotes, pickles, and other pantry props establish that the household already makes varied winter food; they do not count as today's harvest or substitutes for its parcel allocation.

## Reclaim the table

The table under the vine starts with three authored movable props, such as a tool tin and two spare trays. They accept multiple sensible nearby resting places: a side shelf or cupboard. There is no precise alignment puzzle, dirt meter, or requirement to rediscover a tiny missing item.

Clearing the surface and placing the folded cloth can be done as soon as its local access is open, even before Grandpa mentions it. The current world state records the achievement. Later dialogue acknowledges completed work rather than asking for the same action again.

Keep the active processing bench beside this table so preparing the social surface never removes a machine's required output position. Preparing the table early does not spawn new work clutter onto it. Existing work carriers may rest only on their own valid surfaces.

When the player chooses to finish, a short authored transition adds bread, cheese, tomatoes, a small plate of peppers, and glasses. The meal is supplied from the household's existing food and is a presentation of hospitality, not a third harvest-processing output. No cooking actions or crowd simulation are required.

## Finish conditions

The household sketch reflects several views of the same day's work:

| Readiness item | Condition in the proposed game |
| --- | --- |
| Yard and equipment access | All five finite supply areas are clear, and the shed, cellar, and table approaches are usable. |
| Harvest resolved | No raw, carried, queued, processing, uncollected, or temporarily stored harvest remains unresolved. All finished food is on Grandpa's shelves or in the two family parcels. |
| Jars returned | The original empty-jar group has reached its return rack. |
| Family parcels ready | Both boxes meet their targets and are placed at the gate. |
| Table prepared | All three work props are put aside and the cloth is placed. |

When all five conditions hold, show **Ready to finish the day**. The player can inspect, take a photograph, or keep arranging food. Choosing **Finish the day** rechecks the current state, resolves the meal transition, and presents the ending. Do not finish automatically, start a countdown, or add another required delivery.

Optional keepsakes, achievements, the final gifted bottle, and the choice of which compatible pepper product goes to each family box do not gate completion. A player who clears toward the cellar before the wheelbarrow, returns jars early, or shelves everything before making parcels must still be able to finish.

## Pacing limits and later evaluation

Returned jars and the table are short setpieces: one carrier transfer, then three prop moves and a cloth placement. Parcel allocation happens on existing output trips. Target well under five minutes of additional household handling across the full game; this is a tuning cap, not a reason to fill five minutes with chores.

After the core prototype succeeds, test whether participants notice who the food is for, use different task orders, and prefer the meal ending to stopping at the last pile. Track detours and repeated confirmations. If a task adds only a highlighted delivery with no appreciated context or choice, compress it to an authored reveal or cut it.
