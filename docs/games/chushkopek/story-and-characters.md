# Story, characters, and ending

[Design index](readme.md) · original narrative for the household winter-preparation proposal

## Just a few peppers

Grandpa calls the adult player character for help: he cannot manage the peppers alone this year, there are not many, and he will make it worth their while.

The yard immediately contradicts him. Crates lean against the fence. Sacks swallow the shed approach. A tarp stretches across something implausibly large. Beside Grandpa's chair stands a single-slot chushkopek.

The player has arrived for one apparently small favor, and the joke becomes **“While you're here…”**. The larger objective is to get the household ready for winter: clear access, process the main pepper supply, put returned jars away, prepare family food parcels, and restore the table under the vine. The season frames one unhurried Sunday; no calendar or deadline drives play.

## Story told through the household

**The first mismatch.** A few peppers move through the opening demonstration while the player sees the scale of the job. The plastic crate appears quickly. The game acknowledges the absurdity and gives the player something useful.

**Grandpa's missing equipment.** Clearing reveals tools he already owns. A wheel appears before the wheelbarrow is usable; opening an old refrigerator/tool cupboard reveals the grinder kit. His memory is selective, but the upgrades are real. Discovery should alter the next few minutes of play.

**While you're here.** An empty jar carrier sits by the gate and spare work props occupy the vine table. Their meanings emerge through short remarks and familiar handling. The finite household sketch already acknowledges them; completing one before its dialogue should produce recognition, not a repeat instruction.

**Who the food is for.** Two labeled boxes, For Aunt and Take to the city, make the food's recipients tangible. The player allocates compatible finished batches between these parcels and Grandpa's winter shelves. The parcels are part of the existing supply plan, not extra harvest introduced near the end.

**The equipment under the tarp.** Grandpa has built a comically oversized answer to his own quantity problem. Improvised fittings on the starting tray establish this habit; the folded rack develops it when found. The final reveal works whichever equipment path the player takes first. The player reveals it early enough to use it on the final area. Its dramatic silhouette is visible as a promise before the cover comes off, and the next large dump demonstrates a real reduction in work. His explanation treats the invention as an ordinary household convenience; the scale carries the joke.

**The household ready.** The player can see across the property; reserved cellar bays are full, family parcels wait at the gate, and the table is prepared. Choosing Finish the day brings food and family sounds to that table. Grandpa's thank-you accompanies the change from work to hospitality.

## Alternative stories and their place

| Idea | Useful contribution | Current decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passing down a tradition | Makes old tools, a recipe card, and the final gift meaningful. | A light emotional layer; no implied illness or death. |
| Yard industrial revolution | Makes equipment discoveries and batch handling visually funny. | Main progression flavor, capped at one final invented processor. |
| Cellar mystery | Gives a visible destination and a modest unanswered question. | The cellar opens during progression; the final gift remains separate. |
| Hidden reason for the mountain | Reveals Grandpa has promised food to family. | The two named parcels use the existing harvest; no surprise extra quota. |
| While you're here | Makes a few household tasks feel like one familiar family day. | One returned carrier and one table setpiece, not unlimited favors. |
| Pepper championship | Could motivate precision presentation and judging. | Deferred; it would pull attention back toward cooking scores. |

The mystery is primarily what the yard contains and how this much food got here. It does not require a thriller plot or an expensive surprise location.

## Small cast

| Character | Function | Presentation limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grandpa | Companion, short explanations, fixed preparation handoff, owner of improbable equipment. | One seated character, limited authored gestures, a few useful lines. |
| Aunt | Family context, recipe note, recipient of a prepared parcel. | Notes or occasional offscreen voice. |
| Neighbor | Brief remarks from the street/fence; optional favor after completion. | No walking schedule or required visit. |
| Family members | Give the pantry, city parcel, and meal emotional purpose. | Photo, labels, offscreen arrival audio or a short authored ending transition. |

Grandpa's automatic contribution is represented at a fixed preparation bench. It is an abstraction supporting batch processing; the game does not need to simulate his hands working through every pepper or his movement around the yard.

His glass and rakia bottle can remain social props. A small final gift fits the user's preferred atmosphere. There is no drinking buff, camera intoxication effect, or distillation side game.

## Dialogue follows action

The landscape, destination symbols, and household sketch explain the required work. Dialogue adds personality and can be muted without losing instructions. A discovery line should follow the visible reveal and never interrupt carrying.

Budget approximately 24 short lines for the complete small campaign, using the candidates in [Objectives and comedy](jobs-events-and-comedy.md). Play most once, give incidental remarks a long cooldown, and keep silence between them.

The humor comes from scale, resourcefulness, old objects, and Grandpa's estimates. Preserve his competence: his machinery really helps, and his explanation of the next intake should be reliable. Do not make the player tolerate bad instructions for a joke.

## The ending

The ending follows the [household finish conditions](household-readiness-and-parcels.md#finish-conditions): yard access restored, all harvest allocated to cellar/parcels, returned jars put away, both parcels ready at the gate, and the table prepared. Optional keepsakes do not gate it.

Show **Ready to finish the day** when the current conditions hold. Let the player inspect the yard, food, and parcels without taking control of the camera. They deliberately choose **Finish the day**. Machinery is quiet, and a short authored transition turns the cleared, cloth-covered table into a meal with bread, cheese, tomatoes, a small plate of peppers, and glasses. Family presence can be conveyed through offscreen greetings; no crowd AI is needed. The meal uses existing household food, not an extra production quest.

Grandpa then presents the family recipe card or the old single-slot roaster as a keepsake. Beside the final shelf is a small wrapped bottle labeled **„За специални случаи“ / “For special occasions.”** The bottle is a punchline and a thank-you, supported by the substantial reward already visible.

A final original line can acknowledge his understatement:

> „Видя ли? Няколко рафта, нищо работа.“
>
> “See? A few shelves. Hardly any work.”

Allow an optional photograph and closing exchange around the family table. No mandatory task appears after Finish the day. Optional favors belong to a separate replay choice; the household's completed Sunday remains complete.
