# Household objectives, discoveries, and comedy

[Design index](readme.md) · original proposals for Just a few peppers

The main campaign uses five areas in [Yard and progression](yard-and-progression.md), with several reachable priorities after the gate. Objectives point to physical work, useful discoveries, and household destinations. The table below is not an enforced sequence; the player can return jars, prepare the table, or allocate food as soon as the relevant access is available.

## Main objectives

| Objective | What the player does | What changes | Why continue? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Make room at the gate | Gather the small opening pile and feed the supplied tray. | Exposes the plastic crate and a usable worktable. | Try a much larger load immediately. |
| Find the rest of the wheelbarrow | Clear the shallow pocket around its visible wheel. | Frees the carrier and larger feed rack. | A previously familiar pile now takes fewer trips. |
| Get the shed door open | Clear ordinary stock and open the old refrigerator/tool cupboard. | Reveals the grinder kit; it is moved outside in one authored interaction. | A useful household object has an unexpected second purpose. |
| Reach the cellar steps | Remove piles on the apron and open a direct path. | Reveals shelf bays and shortens the storage trip. | Watch the accumulated reserve transfer into a proper winter cellar. |
| Uncover Grandpa's solution | Release the tarp from the cleared side. | Reveals the oversized processor and final supply. | Use the large intake and batch output on the remaining mess. |
| Return the family's jars | Move the one empty-jar carrier from the gate to its rack. | Returned containers are ready for reuse. | A familiar family obligation fits into an existing trip. |
| Pack food for the family | Allocate existing compatible output to two labeled boxes, then place them at the gate. | Grandpa's shelf and family parcels visibly share the harvest. | The work supports named people. |
| Make room to sit | Put three work props aside and place the cloth on the vine table. | A work surface becomes a social space. | The day has a destination beyond the machines. |
| Finish the day | Meet the household readiness conditions and choose the finish action. | Open yard, fuller cellar, parcels ready, family meal, small gift. | A finite project becomes hospitality. |

A tool unlock happens when its physical access pocket is clear, not when every pepper in that area has already been processed. Otherwise the player does all the tedious work before receiving the tool intended to improve it.

## A later section to evaluate, after the core test

The next tiny prototype remains the one-mound test in [Scope and validation](scope-and-validation.md). The later section below adds context only after that test succeeds. Target approximately 10–15 minutes for observation; this is an experiment budget, not a proven pace.

1. Start with a crate, a reachable mound, an operating intake, and a visible output carrier.
2. Gather and tip a few loads. The ground opens where the player works; the machine accepts the full compatible load in one action.
3. A wheel emerges. Clearing around it releases the wheelbarrow and matching feed rack.
4. Give the player another comparable mound. The upgraded tool takes larger bites and fewer delivery trips.
5. Carry a finished jar group toward storage on a return leg. The food display now represents a visible share of the removed mound.
6. Reach the shed threshold and reveal the grinder. End the section with the cleared space, food, and new equipment all visible.

Do not script every transfer to force the schedule. If the section naturally finishes sooner, record it. If it needs ten minutes of featureless walking to reach the reveal, change its layout.

## Discoveries with distinct jobs

| Discovery | Function | Placement rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plastic crate | First handling upgrade. | In the opening accessible pocket. |
| Wheelbarrow and folded rack | Carry and intake/output capacity increase together. | Partial silhouette visible before the player commits to a large pile. |
| Grinder kit | New output route. | Reachable using the existing route and tools. |
| Loading chute / machine cover release | Points toward the last large upgrade. | Accessible through either the cleared shed passage or cellar apron. |
| Old refrigerator/tool cupboard | Holds the grinder kit in a memorable reused object. | Inside the reachable shed bay; opening the door is one action. |
| Curtain over cellar shelves | Reveals inherited stock and spaces for today's food. | One short reveal on opening the cellar; no cleaning or cloth simulation. |
| Family photograph | Explains who used the yard before. | Optional ledge revealed beside the main path. |
| Recipe card | Adds meaning to the lyutenitsa station. | Attached to the grinder, never required as a consumable recipe unlock. |
| Peculiar pepper portrait | A small humorous collection. | Photograph before collection, then process the pepper normally. |

Keep a maximum of three optional keepsakes/portraits in the smallest campaign. These are examples to select from, not separate collections to complete. Mandatory equipment is guaranteed; there is no rare-drop gate or key that can enter a grinder.

## Pepper variety candidates

| Candidate | Possible physical difference | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal peppers | Either finished product after the grinder unlock. | Baseline. |
| Sound misshapen peppers / edible pieces | Route a tagged batch to lyutenitsa. | Baseline second class. |
| Tiny peppers | A dense, lively cascade from the same carrier. | Cosmetic first; a new capacity rule needs evidence. |
| Huge peppers | One amusing visible shape on an ordinary load. | Cosmetic first; do not block progress on a new machine. |
| Hot peppers | Optional clearly separated condiment request. | Deferred; adds another recipe/output and cannot be mixed invisibly. |
| Rare absurd shape | Photograph, then use normally. | Optional authored collectible appearance. |

Normal peppers can make lyutenitsa too. The game's irregular class does not depict rot, spoiled food, or scorched flesh. Do not teach the player that grinding makes unusable food acceptable.

## Physical jokes worth keeping

| Moment | Trigger / action | Payoff | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tiny machine, enormous yard | Opening camera remains player-controlled with both in view. | The scale mismatch explains the joke immediately. | No repeated camera cut. |
| The missing wheelbarrow | Clear around a familiar wheel shape. | Grandpa recognizes something buried in his own supply. | It is an actual useful upgrade. |
| The sack behind the sacks | Clearing opens a view into an already-authored area. | The true scale becomes apparent spatially. | Never spawn a replacement pile in cleared ground. |
| “Only vegetables” tarp | Pull the cover off the final machine. | A patchwork contraption occupies much more space than expected. | One new processor, not a machinery-construction game. |
| Recipe versus hardware | Deliver irregular stock; grinder produces red mash with a brief mechanical flourish. | A domestic recipe has acquired unreasonable equipment. | No repetitive grinding or stirring input. |
| First cellar row | Place a whole jar carrier into a bay. | A neat group fills in beside older preserves. | One batch placement; no individual lid puzzle. |
| The returned empties | Carry the labeled jar group inward while a batch runs. | A gate full of obligations becomes an organized household. | One authored return, not a recurring packaging bottleneck. |
| The cupboard that used to be a fridge | Open it after clearing access. | Food storage has become tool storage. | Use the existing reveal verb; no electrical repair. |
| The table gets its old job back | Clear props and place the cloth. | The final scene occupies the same surface that held work clutter. | Three prop moves and one placement; no cooking or table-cleaning minigame. |
| Decorative bottle | Find Grandpa's labeled thank-you beside the final shelf. | A small affectionate payoff. | Adds to the finished yard; not the only reward. |

The [household specification](household-readiness-and-parcels.md) caps these tasks, defines early completion, and prevents parcel allocation from requiring extra harvest. The premise should remain understandable with all dialogue muted. Favor a few strong physical jokes over a constant commentary stream.

Use the tiny-load joke once, after the next carrier is visibly available. The player should not be mocked while stuck with slow controls. Lines are original drafts; final delivery still benefits from a native Bulgarian review.

The three-line machine exchange is an alternative to the existing handle joke, selected within the same approximately 24-line budget. A player reply can be a subtitle without adding a voiced protagonist or dialogue-choice system. The exchange never blocks the first dump, and the machine must communicate its benefit with dialogue muted.

## Optional favors after the ending

A neighbor can request a small accepted batch using existing routes, or the player can replay a cleared-area scenario with earned equipment. Both clearly identify new supply before starting. They do not repopulate the completed campaign yard without the player's deliberate choice.

Defer precision quality jobs, hot-pepper orders, repair tasks, and chasing loose jars. Those would reintroduce a different central challenge.
