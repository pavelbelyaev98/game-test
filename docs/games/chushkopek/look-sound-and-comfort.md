# Look, sound, and comfort

[Design index](readme.md) · presentation targets for Just a few peppers

The first image should show an impossible quantity of peppers and a very small appliance in the same Bulgarian yard. The final image should show that recognizable property open and usable, reserved cellar bays filled beside older food, family parcels at the gate, and a prepared meal under the vine.

## Make quantity and removal readable

Compose a slightly stylized late-summer backyard with vine shade, enamel basins, wooden/plastic crates, patched outdoor tables, old garden chairs, an exterior power lead, handwritten labels, and a street gate. Add a returned jar carrier, older mismatched jar silhouettes behind a cellar curtain, and a dead refrigerator used as a tool cupboard. The culture lives in these objects and their arrangement before any character speaks. These are selected household details, not a claim that every Bulgarian home looks alike.

Different pile forms create variety with the same material: a broad shallow spill, a compact mound, a stack of filled crates, a slumped sack, and a tarp-covered supply. Each must communicate what the current tool can collect. Do not make the player hunt invisible residual peppers to finish a patch.

Pile depletion should happen near the gather action, revealing a continuous strip of ground or an equipment silhouette. Small surface motion can sell settling; large distant objects should not pop away when the player touches one corner.

Normal and irregular stock use shape and a simple container symbol. Decorative color variation must not imply a new routing rule. Optional odd shapes remain noticeable without sparkling loot beams.

## Prioritize the actions seen hundreds of times

| Action | Visual response | Sound target | Reward |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gather | Small clumps rise into the carrier; the local source shrinks immediately. | Soft pepper contacts, scrape of the container edge. | A satisfying bite out of the visible problem. |
| Fill carrier | Contents rise to readable capacity; grip/body pose changes slightly. | Increasingly dense contacts, brief full cue. | A load that visibly feels substantial. |
| Tip | The carrier follows the player's deliberate input; a short cascade lands in the intake. | Distinct rattles, impacts, empty-container tail. | Physical release of the whole collected load. |
| Machine operates | Intake feeds; short roast/rest/preparation stages remain visible. | Sizzle, clunks, brief steam release. | A lively response to what the player delivered. |
| Lyutenitsa runs | Grinder makes mash; adjoining finishing stage handles the rest. | Short mechanical rhythm, soft output sound. | A different material transformation. |
| Allocate a food carrier | Jars settle into a shelf bay or a named family box. | Restrained glass contacts and a placement sound. | Visible food for home or for a relative. |
| Return the jar carrier | The empty group settles onto its rack, leaving the gate clear. | A familiar group clink, short crate scrape. | Household order using a known interaction. |
| Reclaim the table | Work props move to side storage; the cloth settles in one authored placement. | Tool tin contact and a soft fabric movement. | The workday gains a place to end. |
| Uncover equipment | A wheel, handle, or machine edge emerges before full access. | Subtle reveal cue followed by original banter. | Anticipation, discovery, and a usable upgrade. |

Readiness sounds signal something available, never something about to fail. Smoke rises into open air and clears targets quickly. The final machine can be louder and more animated briefly, but it must not obscure the work area continuously.

All processing remains outdoors on the open-sided terrace. The shed contains stored equipment; the cellar contains finished food and the gift. The invented bulk machinery may look patched together while retaining enclosed working parts and clear input/output openings. This is an art direction, not a buildable electrical design.

## Readable representation within solo scope

Try authored pile stages, grouped contents, and a small pool of moving pepper visuals before considering unrestricted physics. The prototype must test transitions from gathering to carried load to cascade to settled contents. If that sequence looks disconnected, the central fantasy is not yet supported.

Use one crate, one wheelbarrow, and shared jar groups rather than unique assets for every batch. A carrier can show representative contents while a concise amount indicator records exact capacity. Food transferred from the outdoor rack to a shelf or parcel should visibly leave the first display. Existing pantry props remain distinguishable from today's allocated food. Use clear shelf/box labels, not separate visual production systems for every winter dish.

The final processor reuses recognizable shapes from the ordinary appliance, rack, and grinder. It needs one memorable cover reveal and convincing operation, not simulated components.

## Comfortable handling

- Broad collection and tipping targets; visible feedback immediately after valid input.
- Adjustable sensitivity/FOV, invert-look choice, clear text, and separate dialogue/effects/ambience volumes.
- Stable camera, unobstructed forward view, head bob and shake off by default.
- Hold/toggle alternatives for gathering and tipping; no rapid clicking requirement.
- Wheelbarrow turning, reversing, and ordinary paths tested for comfort. No balancing or driving minigame.
- Cancel/recover returns the existing carrier and its contents to a valid nearby place.
- Pause during menus, instruction screens, and loss of application focus.
- Optional destination hints and readable symbols; routing does not depend on color alone.

Useful status words are **Collect / Carrier full / Tip load / Working / Output full / Ready to store**. The household sketch distinguishes yard access, resolved harvest, food allocation, returned jars, parcels, and table readiness. It shows useful states rather than another large spreadsheet. Avoid per-pepper cooking timers and dense production dashboards.

A stationary work mode from the old cooking proposal would no longer represent the main traversal activity. Instead investigate shorter routes, simpler carrier movement, and generous target reach as comfort options.

## Atmosphere and candidate clips

Use original or licensed radio material, a gate latch, birds, distant neighbors, and long quiet intervals. Important action feedback takes priority over banter and radio.

Three short candidate clips explain this version: a wheelbarrow emerging from a shrinking mound; one large dump into the absurd processor; the cluttered worktable compared with the final family table, with winter food and parcels visible nearby. They depend on actual gameplay feeling good. They are not evidence of demand or a guarantee of shareability.
