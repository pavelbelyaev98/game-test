# Jobs, small events, and comedy

[Design index](readme.md) · all jobs and lines below are original proposals

The main campaign introduces one new decision at a time. Quantity is finite and disclosed. A “beautiful result” is optional; the family accepts an ordinary completed batch.

## Eight main jobs

These counts are starting values for pacing, not real preserving yields. One game jar uses three prepared pepper units, including the bounded spread recipe. [Mechanics](core-loop-and-mechanics.md) defines recovery and the preserving handoff.

| ID / job | Required result | New player decision | Completion payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J1. Before lunch | Three peppers on the lunch plate. | Learn readiness and one complete handling cycle. | Shared lunch beat; first packing area opens. |
| J2. Two jars, honestly | Six peppers → two roasted-pepper jars. | Learn the whole pack, handoff, label, store sequence. | First shelf row; triple roaster revealed. |
| J3. The good machine | Nine peppers → three roasted-pepper jars. | Choose simultaneous or staggered loading. | Second bowl and larger bowl capacity. |
| J4. Two pots, one brain | Nine peppers → three roasted-pepper jars. | Decide when to close a bowl and switch to the other. | Tray transfer and optional peeling assistance. |
| J5. Aunt's nice peppers | Twelve peppers → four roasted-pepper jars. | Read ordinary/thin pepper timing; intact appearance is an optional bonus. | Label guide and parcel positions. |
| J6. Whose are these? | Twelve peppers → four roasted-pepper jars, two for each named recipient. | Organize destinations while the roast routine is familiar. | Prepared recipe kit and, conditionally, grinder/pan. |
| J7. The family recipe | Twelve peppers → four lyutenitsa jars. | Discover grinding and the short consistency interaction. | A second visual product on the pantry display. |
| J8. These really are the last | Fifteen peppers → three roasted-pepper jars and two lyutenitsa jars. | Allocate good prepared peppers between two known products. | Complete family boxes, cellar key, ending. |

The main sequence totals **78 peppers, one three-pepper lunch plate, and 25 jars** before replacements. No job secretly adds to those totals.

If the lyutenitsa test fails, J7 becomes a four-jar presentation-and-recipient remix, and J8 becomes five roasted-pepper jars divided among the last family boxes. Quantities and ending stay the same. Do not replace the removed branch with a new multi-station recipe during the same evaluation.

## Optional job remixes

Offer at most four of these in the small-game scope, after the ending or through clearly optional cards. They reuse the existing verbs. No new NPC system or unique ingredient chain is required.

| Remix | Interesting constraint | Why it might be fun | Cut if… |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The crooked crate | One visible curved shape may need one reposition. | A small observation test using familiar handling. | Repositioning becomes obligatory clicking without judgment. |
| Thick and thin | Mix the two already readable timing families. | Staggering matters more than identical timers. | Audio/visual cues cannot support a relaxed pace. |
| Presentation jars | Optional intact-pepper target on a normal job. | Pride in the appearance of finished work. | Mouse precision causes unfair quality loss. |
| A box for each cousin | Several clearly labeled destinations, same product. | Watch an untidy job resolve into ordered parcels. | It becomes memorization or administrative work. |
| One roaster, unhurried | Replay a small job using one active socket. | Supports players who enjoy a slower ritual. | It is needed to grind a mandatory reward. |
| The clean finish | Empty receiving pots and tidy the tray with one broad sweep. | A short punctuation after the food is already complete. | Cleaning grows into another progress-bar game. |

## Funny mechanics worth trying

| Idea | Trigger and player action | Payoff | Boundaries / status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The prestigious triple roaster | Reveal the cloth-covered machine after J2; inspect and use it. | Comically formal unveiling followed by a real improvement. | Core story beat. No real car logo or branded model name. |
| Grandpa's estimate | Hear his understatement while the board shows the accepted count. | The visible mismatch makes the joke. | Core banter. The actual counter remains accurate. |
| Sack behind the sack | A new sightline reveals an upcoming chapter's labeled supply. | One escalating visual gag. | Once in campaign; never refill a completed crate. |
| The honorary quality controller | Put the opening lunch plate beside Grandpa. | He takes the credit very seriously. | Scripted only; no stealing required jars. |
| Professional batch handoff | Give Grandpa the announced remainder after peeling one pepper. | The helper who was chatting now saves the player work. | Conditional upgrade; fixed station, no autonomous simulation. |
| Lid diplomacy | One misplaced lid has its matching jar marked nearby; swap it once. | Familiar domestic annoyance resolves with a crisp fit. | Optional one-off; never repeated random lid mismatch. |
| Pepper portrait | Photograph one of three authored odd shapes before cooking it. | A ridiculous small “hall of fame.” | Optional; photo preserves the memory without removing food. |
| Laundry complaint | An offscreen remark prompts Grandpa to move one hanging towel in a short scripted beat. | Neighborhood atmosphere responds to the work. | Cosmetic, optional; no smoke-routing puzzle or penalty. |
| A borrowed pot returns | An optional job reward arrives with a neighbor's note attached. | The yard's tools acquire tiny stories. | Use an existing pot model; no collection debt system. |
| Final shelf photo | Frame the pantry and empty work area after J8. | A shareable image of work the player actually did. | Proposed screenshot moment; no social-account integration. |

The most promising combination is **prestigious roaster + honest estimate gag + useful Grandpa handoff + final shelf photo**. These reinforce the same game. The other ideas are substitutes or cheap accents, not a requirement to build ten event systems.

## Original Bulgarian line bank

English versions communicate the intended joke; they are localization drafts, not literal translation requirements. Have a native speaker review final delivery and regional voice.

| Trigger | Bulgarian draft | English intent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival | „Само тия са. Другите са за после.“ | “Only these. The others are for later.” |
| Reading the job list | „Аз броя щайгите. Ти защо броиш чушките?“ | “I count crates. Why are you counting peppers?” |
| First good roast | „Черна е кожата. Отдолу е наградата.“ | “The skin's black. The reward is underneath.” |
| Early removal | „Тая още се запознава с чушкопека.“ | “That one's still getting acquainted with the roaster.” |
| Triple-roaster reveal | „Три места. Никой не стои прав.“ | “Three seats. Nobody has to stand.” |
| First full load | „Ей сега вече работим по европейски.“ | “Now we've got an international operation.” |
| Larger bowl | „Малката тенджера беше само за увереност.“ | “The little pot was just for building confidence.” |
| Player finds a sack | „Не е скрит. На сянка е.“ | “It's not hidden. It's in the shade.” |
| Helpful handoff | „Давай насам. И аз съм белил преди интернет.“ | “Pass them here. I peeled peppers before the internet.” |
| First neat jar | „Тоя отпред. Да го виждат гостите.“ | “Put that one at the front. Let the guests see it.” |
| Wrong label | „Чушките са наши. Етикетът е на съседа.“ | “Our peppers. The neighbor's label.” |
| Lyutenitsa starts | „Сега вече и хляб ще трябва.“ | “Now we're going to need bread as well.” |
| Waste recovered | „Сложи друга. Тая си взе почивен ден.“ | “Get another. That one's taken the day off.” |
| Quiet stretch | „Радиото да говори. Ние си знаем работата.“ | “Let the radio talk. We know what we're doing.” |
| Last main crate accepted | „Тая е последната. Даже го написах.“ | “This one's the last. I even wrote it down.” |
| Finished display | „Тия рафтове сами ли се напълниха?“ | “Did those shelves fill themselves?” |

Use the recovery joke once at most; repeated mistakes should receive useful feedback and silence rather than ridicule. Do not play banter over the first readiness cue or while the player is learning a new action.
