# Look, sound, and comfort

[Design index](readme.md) · presentation targets for later experiments

The first screenshot should communicate **outside, family yard, peppers, distinctive appliance**. The strongest final image should show the same space transformed by the player's work.

## Visual identity

Choose a readable, slightly stylized late-summer backyard: worn plaster, vine shade, a patched table covering, enamel bowls, wooden crates, glass jars, and the street visible through a gate. Keep wear affectionate and useful for atmosphere; do not turn every surface into a cleaning task.

Warm daylight and red pepper flesh are the focal colors. Use darker charred skin, pale bowl interiors, and clear work surfaces to separate interactable objects from the background. Thin/ordinary pepper differences must include silhouette and a small crate symbol, not just red versus green.

The roaster should remain recognizable as a compact cylindrical appliance. The [research](research-and-authenticity.md) grounds the chamber, lid, and tong handling. Any extra readiness indicator is a game aid, not a claim that the real device has one.

The outdoor rule applies to every campaign chapter. A cellar shot may show stored food and the reward, but the active workshop never migrates indoors. Smoke and heat distortion remain small and localized; the yard should not resemble an industrial fire.

## Feedback priorities

| Action | Visual response | Sound target | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Take/insert pepper | Confident tong grip and short seating motion. | Light tool contact; initial sizzle. | Input feels accepted immediately. |
| Approach readiness | Blisters, lifted skin, subtle softened form. | Sizzle changes; distinguishable restrained pop. | Supports judgment while looking elsewhere. |
| Inspect | Pepper rises clearly above the rim; skin and body are readable. | Short tong/lid contact. | The player can answer “ready yet?” without guessing. |
| Open bowl | Brief steam release, then an unobstructed batch. | Soft lid clink and short hiss. | Builds anticipation and delivers a reveal. |
| Peel | Broad skin strip curls and releases; glossy flesh remains. | Light papery crackle and soft release. | The physical transformation is the reward. |
| Fill jar | Pepper folds into a stable visible arrangement. | Soft contact against glass. | Loose food becomes ordered finished goods. |
| Label/store | Label lies flat; jar snaps gently into its place. | Paper brush, restrained glass contact. | Makes completion legible without a loud reward screen. |
| Grind, if retained | Mash gathers into a visibly different material. | Short mechanical rhythm. | The second product earns a distinct texture and sound. |

Prioritize these interactions before bespoke NPC faces, detailed streets, or decorative clutter. Surface wetness should make peppers appetizing without giving them rubbery or plastic highlights. Food arrangement can be authored; believable motion matters more than unrestricted physics.

Do not use a lid “pop” as proof that food was safely preserved. The explicit family handoff and passage of time communicate that a separate stage happened; the satisfying player action is packing and labeling.

## Comfortable first-person work

- Adjustable sensitivity, field of view, invert-look choice, and separate audio volumes.
- Stable camera; head bob, camera shake, and forced zoom off by default.
- Broad interaction targets with concise names and compatible-destination highlights.
- Pause all timers in menus, explanations, and loss of application focus.
- Keep subtitles clear of work targets; dialogue can be reduced without losing instructions.
- Optional readiness text/icons and a slower-roasting mode with equal rewards.
- Toggle alternatives for prolonged holds; no rapid clicking or sustained precision dragging.
- A work-position option can reduce movement for players who prefer operating from the table; it must still give access to every required station.

Status words should name meaningful states: **Needs roasting, Ready to lift, Resting, Ready to peel, For preserving, Ready to label**. Avoid a screen full of percentage bars. Optional indicators should explain uncertainty without removing the player's ability to inspect and learn.

## Atmosphere and shareable moments

Use original or properly licensed short radio material, distant yard sounds, birds, a gate latch, and occasional offscreen conversation. Duck ambience beneath readiness cues. Silence between remarks is part of the atmosphere.

Three candidate clips can explain the game without narration: lift and peel a pepper; unveil the triple roaster and smoothly work a batch; compare the crate-filled yard with the final jar display. Record them only when the corresponding interactions actually exist. No promise that these clips will go viral or prove demand.
