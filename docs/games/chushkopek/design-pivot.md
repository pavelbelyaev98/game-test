# Design decisions: Just a few peppers

[Design index](readme.md) · revision history through the household winter-preparation proposal, September 5, 2026

## Current revision: a household project

The pacing and machinery refinement makes the household limit explicit: target 2–3 minutes of added required handling, with a hard ceiling below five minutes. Roughly 80–90% or more of active play should remain in pepper handling, discoveries, routing, and food storage, with no fixed 20-minute clearing blocks. Grandpa's existing final machine gains a clearer three-stage progression at one outdoor station; each stage must reduce effort across feeding, processing, and output collection. This refines the current proposal without expanding the next prototype. [Pacing definition](household-readiness-and-parcels.md#pacing-limits-and-later-evaluation), [equipment stages](yard-and-progression.md#grandpas-three-equipment-stages).

The title is **Just a few peppers**, as requested. The [new comparable-game memo](../../../Ideas/Just_A_Few_Peppers_Comparable_Game_Case_Studies.md), [Bulgarian culture memo](research/Just_A_Few_Peppers_Bulgarian_Culture_and_Game_Direction.md), and latest supplied conversation broaden the goal from an empty pepper yard to a household ready for winter. Bulk handling remains the main repeatable activity.

| Previous bulk-clearing proposal | Current household proposal |
| --- | --- |
| All harvest ends on cellar shelves. | Current harvest is allocated between Grandpa's shelves and two named family parcels. |
| Empty jars are background supply. | One returned carrier becomes a short, culturally meaningful handling task. |
| Five areas imply a mostly sequential route. | After the gate, several access pockets offer different priorities. |
| End at a clear yard and complete stockpile. | Also reclaim the vine table, then choose Finish the day and share the meal. |
| More visual material carries most of the progression. | Useful discoveries and a few changes of purpose give the same property variety. |

[Household readiness and parcels](household-readiness-and-parcels.md) is the source for allocation, one-off tasks, and completion. Added tasks reuse carrying and placement. Other winter foods can appear on shelves or at the table without introducing recipes for compote, turshiya, or distilling.

The five new comparisons give these design constraints:

| Comparison in the supplied memo | Decision taken into the specs |
| --- | --- |
| Food Processing Simulator | A second output must change a meaningful decision; test the entire carrier workflow, not just nominal capacity. |
| How to Make an Atomic Bomb in Your Garden | Keep the absurd household project, but allow local task order and avoid progression dependent on unstable carried props. |
| My Summer Car | Express culture through objects and activities within one property; do not copy a large life-simulation scope. |
| Barn Finders | Favor a few useful, memorable discoveries over a collectible beneath every pile. |
| Mon Bazou | Each added chore must visibly serve the household; remove tasks that only increase a completion number. |

Selected Food Processing reviews independently describe repeated processing chains across different products. Selected reviews of the garden-project game describe hauling fatigue, constrained actions, and carrier/progress failures. These are concrete reported failure modes, not evidence that all players agree or a complete review census. [Food Processing reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3928990/negativereviews/?browsefilter=toprated), [Garden-project reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3700980/negativereviews/?browsefilter=toprated&l=english)

The memos' reception figures are dated source notes, not fresh measurements or a forecast for this game. Their original wording is preserved in the supplied research files. The specs adopt design lessons without depending on those percentages.

The prototype remains **one mound, crate, automatic processor, output carrier, storage point, and partly exposed wheelbarrow**. Household setpieces, additional routes, full narrative, and the final machine are not added to that test. A fourfold carrier capacity is not a promise of fourfold total speed.

## Earlier revision: cooking to bulk handling

**The bulk candidate made a load of peppers the meaningful unit of work.** The player clears space, discovers equipment, improves handling, and sees winter food accumulate. This gives the same object immediate, medium-term, and whole-game consequences.

The previous design made roast timing the main skill and expanded into peeling, bowl management, and manual packing. That was coherent as a cooking game, but it left the user's desired scale fantasy weak. Increasing socket counts could have produced more monitoring instead of a satisfying improvement in power.

## What the earlier bulk proposal changed

| Earlier cooking proposal | Bulk-clearing revision |
| --- | --- |
| Judge individual readiness and remove in time. | Collect and route loads; cooking completes automatically. |
| A small ordered workday with eight job cards. | An immediately absurd, finite mess across five connected yard areas. |
| Capacity grants follow completed recipes. | Clearing exposes equipment that changes how the next pile is handled. |
| Manual peel and jar gestures repeat. | Preparation and preserving are compressed into visible batch processing. |
| Cellar is primarily an ending reveal. | Cellar filling is a progress display once its entrance is cleared; a small final gift remains. |
| Modest quantities represent a family supply. | Large visible piles are the problem; the game avoids simulating every displayed pepper independently. |

## What the comparisons support

Leaf it Alone's official description connects clearing leaves with upgraded tools, a changing yard, and things uncovered. That supports the proposed relationship between removal, capacity, and spatial discovery. [Official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3981100/)

Cash Cleaner's official description includes varied physical deliveries, processing equipment, handling upgrades, and secrets in the workspace. That supports variety at the scale of containers and batches. It still includes inspection and handling; the comparison does not establish that every individual action is absent or that unattended machinery alone is entertaining. [Official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2488370/)

A Game About Digging a Hole links collecting, equipment upgrades, further access, and a mystery. We can borrow that sequence of motivations without requiring currency, underground excavation, or a copied ending. [Official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3244220/)

These are documented structures and our design inferences. They do not prove which features caused those games' reception, or that substituting peppers will produce a successful game.

## Bulk decisions retained in the household proposal

| Suggestion | Decision and reason |
| --- | --- |
| Huge visible problem, useful things underneath, oversized handling upgrades | Adopt as the central proposal. Every cleared area should provide space, food, and something worth seeing. |
| No careful burning/roast-timer challenge | Adopt. Machine completion is a reward cue; finished food never deteriorates while the player explores. |
| Two peppers by hand at the start | Keep only for a few transfers, targeting well under two minutes. A miserable tutorial is not required to appreciate an upgrade. |
| Crate → basket → wheelbarrow → chute → homemade machine | Keep crate, wheelbarrow, chute, and final processor. Defer the basket unless it adds a distinct action. |
| Triple chushkopek as a bulk upgrade | Keep its recognizable three-pepper chamber; a fictional loading rack queues larger containers through it. Do not imply a real triple appliance roasts a wheelbarrow all at once. |
| Five or six pepper classes | Start with normal and sound but misshapen peppers. Reserve hot/tiny/huge classes for later evidence; each can multiply routing and asset work. |
| Damaged peppers become lyutenitsa | Define these as misshapen or sound edible pieces. Rotten produce and burnt flesh are not a recipe route. Both products can use good peppers. |
| Grinder produces finished jars | Show a compressed preparation/cooking/preserving handoff around it. The grinder itself visibly produces mash; it does not magically preserve food. |
| More piles behind earlier piles | Adopt as authored spatial discovery. The total exists from the start and the game never refills cleared areas. |
| Every pile contains treasure | Use a few authored equipment reveals and optional keepsakes. Do not depend on random drops or endless collection. |
| Questionable homemade machine | Keep the comic silhouette and patchwork finish. It is invented game equipment, with no engineering or distilling tutorial. |

## The unresolved risk

This could become **walking a container between two markers**. Bigger piles and funny machinery do not fix an unpleasant scoop, unresponsive dump, or long empty return journey.

The next useful experiment is therefore a small pile, a crate, one intake, a visible output, and a wheelbarrow discovery. Test repeated collection and tipping before adding a large map, recipe variety, or collectible rewards. Compare the same quantity before and after the upgrade; make processing keep up so saved hauling time is not replaced by standing at a machine.

The [validation plan](scope-and-validation.md) now tests this hypothesis. The [archived proposal](archive/roasting-v1/readme.md) and existing Unity spike remain available for reference. No code or production bootstrap is part of this revision.
