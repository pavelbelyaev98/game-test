# Assessment of the proposed gameplay additions

This assessment maps proposed additions to their current owners. Bottle and rock physical release is integrated through `110`/`114`; `115`/`116` own current recognition and optional lift/drop/throw. Other optional mechanics remain proposals. The user's **enjoyable vertical flight and dull horizontal return comparison refers to Meltopia, not this game**; `101` separately retains actual current-build handling verdicts and the `116` retest. Free recharge and ordinary physical return remain the baselines.

| Idea | Recommendation and concrete candidate | Main risk / decision owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physics on discovered items | Current bottles and rocks anchor in soil, then fall/tip/settle; `115`/`116` add optional stable handling. `96` compares remaining categories against that baseline | Chasing rolling loot, lost finds and obstructed tunnels can erase the benefit. [96](../development/tasks/96-discovery-physics-design.md) owns further category decisions; full physics on every object is not recommended |
| Some uniques make sound before discovery | Worth adding to content design for a few objects with a believable source: an occasional buried clock tick or mechanical hum, for example | Sound must not label every unique, become an exact treasure locator or demand headphones. [40](../development/tasks/40-discovery-roster.md) chooses examples with `57`/`10`; actual sounds need their normal approval |
| Fuel refill item | Worth comparing an optional limited field charge that buys one more dig or ascent, using the existing shared battery | A mandatory consumable shopping list or portable infinite fuel would weaken the loop. [98](../development/tasks/98-refill-economy-design.md) compares found/paid packs, capacity and use rules |
| Refills should cost money | Discuss before implementation. A paid refill may create a real spending choice, but it may merely tax every expedition. Compare free base refill, priced energy actually added and a safe basic-charge fallback | Finite loot plus compulsory running costs can strand a save or make rescue cheaper than service. `98` owns the charge policy; `59` owns rescue consequences. My initial recommendation is free base service with optional paid field convenience unless priced service proves more enjoyable |
| E to collect uniques; purpose or sale | A deliberate special action remains a candidate while ordinary finds keep fluid LMB collection. Unique purpose, frequency and sale/retention have been reopened for [117](../development/tasks/117-unique-find-purpose-design.md) | [97](../development/tasks/97-special-find-interaction-design.md) consumes that decision for input/reward transfer before code. Battle equipment is rejected; a purpose need not be a buff or quest for every item |
| Treasure chests | Worth designing as occasional discoveries: expose, deliberately open, see a meaningful authored reward | Avoid key hunts, random-reward rerolls, repeated opening delays or a money reward already irrelevant when found. `97` owns interaction/transactions; `40` owns roster/counts. Chest contents count in existing content/economy budgets |
| Horizontal return travel is dull in Meltopia | Worth an early comparison here. Separate surface walking, known lateral tunnel retracing, navigation confusion and collision friction | There is no reported failure in our build. [100](../development/tasks/100-movement-excavation-playtest.md)/[101](../development/tasks/101-collection-return-playtest.md) establish whether it occurs; [99](../development/tasks/99-return-mobility-design.md) compares remedies only for a demonstrated need |
| Placeable reusable teleporter | Worth a concrete comparison, with one deployed anchor paired to the base as a bounded candidate | It can remove enjoyable ascent and make battery/jetpack purchases irrelevant. `99` compares physical travel with a reusable endpoint and **one** limiter: cooldown OR credits OR uses. Stacking all three is not a recommendation; waiting for a cooldown is not gameplay |
| A reason to use dynamite with a strong shovel | Strengthen existing [71](../development/tasks/71-dynamite-design-research.md): compare an optional planned broad blast or multi-charge excavation shape against the strongest shovel | Do not nerf the shovel or invent bomb-only walls to justify it. If placement/setup/cost never beats the shovel in a useful situation, omit dynamite |

## Suggested order and the feel to preserve

The [status entry point](../development/status.md) owns current priority: UI review/delivery `106`/`107`/`118`, current-build observations `100`/`101`, unique-purpose design `117`, then coordinated style/content preparation. `96`/`97` consume actual feel and purpose decisions before `40` finalizes named finds. Optional refill/travel comparisons `98`/`99` follow the production trip `15`; diagnose actual current defects through the playtest owners without presuming a teleporter. C4 (the selected name for our earlier dynamite proposal) remains a keep/defer/omit decision in `71`.

Further bounded comparisons: [119](../development/tasks/119-backpack-inspection-release-design.md) richer optional inspection/drop-from-backpack, and [120](../development/tasks/120-powered-excavation-feel-design.md) continuous late shovel removal. The requested bottom backpack/key cue is delivery `118`, independent of those optional mechanics and future C4. Underground expansion currently means material/texture areas under `58`; caves are not planned.

The game should create curiosity, growing physical capability and occasional absurd surprises. Peaceful excavation is one part of that, not a requirement that every moment be passive or cozy. A collectible can justify itself through recognition, humor and the personal display without providing a mechanical upgrade. A new cost should create an understandable choice; a convenience should remove a demonstrated dull action while preserving enjoyable ones.

## Evidence and limits

The [634-review audit](steam-review-audit/report.md) supports testing whole-trip purchase value, recognizable rewards, finite-income recovery and voluntary hole shaping. A [positive Hole review](https://steamcommunity.com/id/Synikx/recommended/3244220/) specifically reports bouncing dynamite and repetitive sound while enjoying stronger digging and reshaping the hole; it supports testing control and fatigue, not adding general object physics by itself. The proposed solutions above are design judgments, not proven effects.

Inspected implementation context: [BuriedFind](../../unity/Assets/Runtime/Interaction/BuriedFind.cs) and `FindPhysics` retain item identity and the bottle/rock physical lifecycle; the [collection contract](../features/backlog/discovery-collection.md) owns current `115`/`116` timing/handling. [SurfaceRecharge](../../unity/Assets/Runtime/Player/SurfaceRecharge.cs) refills without a wallet transaction. [FpsPlayer](../../unity/Assets/Runtime/Player/FpsPlayer.cs) uses the same planar walking/sprint speed in flight; thrust changes vertical motion. These code/contract observations do not establish that a new mechanic is necessary.

Use the [playtest list](../development/playtesting.md) to mark actual feelings. Missing production art/audio or an unimplemented proposed mechanic is **NOT READY**, not a failed player verdict. Only a reviewed design decision should replace the relevant concept/feature rule or create its implementation task.
