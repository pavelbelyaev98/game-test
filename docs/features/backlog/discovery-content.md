## Item tiers and frequency

- **Selected:** common/minor means routine finds that feel natural to uncover repeatedly, such as bottles and plain rocks. Familiarity alone does not make an object common. A toy car belongs above common; the earlier common-starter recommendation is withdrawn.
- Common finds supply low-key recognition, satisfying collection and baseline income. Higher tiers supply progressively stronger discovery moments. Do not demand a personal story from every bottle or use frequent special-looking belongings to disguise weak common pickup feel.
- **Reopened, undecided:** [117](../../development/tasks/117-unique-find-purpose-design.md) owns the reason for unique finds, total/type/copy policy and sale versus retention. The user is considering one special find per map, multiple distinct types or repeated copies; none is selected. Existing sale-plus-photo and roster-count directions are comparison baselines. `40` then owns named assignments, per-item frequency/value and allocation under that decision. Higher-tier status alone never implies utility, E interaction, detector eligibility, colored labels or one-per-save uniqueness.
- **Selected exclusion:** collectibles do not become battle equipment. Combat utility and fear of selling a future weapon are rejected directions; `117` evaluates rewards within excavation, discovery and the existing optional passive/ending systems.
