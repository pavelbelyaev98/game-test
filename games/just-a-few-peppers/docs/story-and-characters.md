# Story, characters, and ending

[Design index](readme.md) · Just a few peppers · current narrative

## The small favor

Grandpa asks the adult player character to help with “just a few peppers.” The amount of harvest around an otherwise accessible yard immediately contradicts him. A modest apparatus waits on the outdoor workbench; increasingly excessive attachments show how he plans to solve this ordinary family task.

This is one unhurried autumn Sunday of family winter preparation. The food is for Grandpa and relatives in the city. That explains the quantity and gives finished work a purpose without adding errands or a seasonal deadline.

## The story follows food and inventions

**The scale mismatch.** A very small appliance sits beside a very large problem. The crate is available immediately, so the joke leads into a useful load instead of a slow tutorial.

**Equipment Grandpa improves.** A visible bench presents useful conversions. The player earns an equipment budget by finishing food, chooses an improvement and sees its effect on the next batch. Grandpa supplies whole improbable attachments without sending the player to find components or clear a buried wheel.

**A lived-in household.** The open shed view includes an old refrigerator used as a tool cupboard, with a grinder inside. Returned jars, labels and photos suggest years of work. Portable props may be arranged or played with, without collection or household obligations.

**Who needs all this food.** Boxes labelled For Aunt and Take to the city are visible at the gate. As the player deposits finished carriers at the one rack, the cellar and boxes fill through [authored presentation](household-readiness-and-parcels.md). Grandpa can mention the recipients once. Nobody hands out another quota.

**Grandpa's solution.** A purchased powered conversion turns the familiar apparatus into an excessive machine he treats as ordinary. It changes useful physical operation and batch/output handling while enough food remains for repeated use. A tarp can be an installation flourish, never an exploration gate.

**Food for winter.** The final handoff resolves the finite food job. The stocked cellar and family parcels show the accomplishment; the now less crowded yard is a consequence, not a renovation objective. No required ending sequence or further errand follows.

## Small cast and dialogue

Keep one seated Grandpa near the processing terrace and vine table, with limited authored gestures. Family presence comes through labels, a photo, a few lines, occasional non-verbal grunts, and optional offscreen greetings. No one needs to walk jars to shelves or follow a work schedule.

His rakia bottle and glass can remain social props; an apparatus could add atmosphere. Playable rakia or lyutenitsa is an explicit [future activity decision](scope-and-validation.md#additional-processing-activities) after the pepper loop is proven. The current narrative adds no production chain, drinking buff or intoxication system.

Use at most about 24 short lines from [Objectives and comedy](jobs-events-and-comedy.md). Batch/purchase/installation comments follow the action and never interrupt operation, gate equipment or require a conversation. Give incidental remarks long quiet gaps. All required interaction remains understandable with dialogue muted; no full voice acting is a prerequisite.

Selected original labels, optional messages/static fictional assistant snippets and brief authored reactions share that writing budget under the [comic-variety contract](jobs-events-and-comedy.md#optional-messages-and-comic-variety). Vary delivery and leave messages readable later without replies or chores. The prototype may include at most one inexpensive static gag at the upgrade test; this does not introduce Grandpa, a computer/mail system or a real chatbot earlier.

Use English-only draft dialogue and prop text during the current design/implementation process. Eventual localization and native review may refine delivery later; no localization system or specification is in this first slice. Grandpa and his dialogue are absent from the first M1–M2 interaction prototype.

Preserve Grandpa's competence. His estimates are funny, but his equipment helps and his directions are reliable. Humor comes from scale, ingenuity, and familiar objects rather than repeated bad instructions.

## Harvest completion

Follow the [finish conditions](household-readiness-and-parcels.md#finish-conditions): all initial food prepared and permanently handed off, with no remaining food in any owner. Yard neatness, unspent Coins and unpurchased equipment add no requirements. There are no separate parcel, table, returned-jar, or discovery checks.

The final valid deposit commits completion, shows a quiet nonmodal acknowledgement, and leaves movement and camera control available. Machines settle into idle, the completed food displays stay visible, and the player can remain in the transformed yard until leaving through normal pause/menu controls. There is no Finish Day button, required table visit, forced cutscene, immediate credits, or menu ejection.

The cellar and waiting parcels show the outcome of the player's harvest. No extra food, chore, deadline, or surprise delivery follows completion.

A short thank-you or a simple table/gift visual may remain a later, cuttable presentation flourish. If used, bread, cheese, tomatoes, a small plate of peppers, glasses, and a wrapped bottle labelled **“For special occasions”** come from existing household supplies. They add no interaction, cinematic system, inventory, reward replay, or completion flag.

An original closing line can be:

> “See? A few shelves. Hardly any work.”

The authored harvest ends here, while the completed yard remains playable. No additional favors, surprise delivery, or new pile follows.
