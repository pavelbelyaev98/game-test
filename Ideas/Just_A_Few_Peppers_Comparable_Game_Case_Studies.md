# Case Studies for Just a Few Peppers
## Food Processing Simulator, How to Make an Atomic Bomb in Your Garden, My Summer Car, Barn Finders, and Mon Bazou

**Research date:** September 5, 2026  
**Purpose:** study games adjacent to the revised *Just a Few Peppers* direction: processing, absurd domestic projects, culturally specific life simulation, discovery, and physical work.

# Executive conclusion

The research points toward a specific target for *Just a Few Peppers*:

> **Do not make a cooking simulator and do not make a factory simulator. Make a short culturally specific physical-comedy game with one clear household project, strong spatial transformation, escalating tools, and a finite ending.**

The most useful lessons are:

- **Food Processing Simulator:** automation/progression is compelling, but changing the input graphic is not content if every product follows the same line.
- **How to Make an Atomic Bomb in Your Garden:** an absurd one-sentence premise is powerful, but humor cannot excuse repetitive hauling, rigid scripted interactions, softlocks, or lack of agency.
- **My Summer Car:** cultural specificity becomes globally appealing when expressed through objects, chores, environment, sounds, and rules, not explanatory text.
- **Barn Finders:** bizarre discoveries can motivate ordinary work, but jokes/items need a strong progression spine or the loop becomes monotonous.
- **Mon Bazou:** mundane jobs work when they clearly serve a larger personal dream; grind kills that relationship.

# 1. Food Processing Simulator

Steam:
https://store.steampowered.com/app/3928990/Food_Processing_Simulator/

Developer: Bewolba Studios  
Release: May 14, 2026  
Early Access began: December 18, 2025

At research time Steam showed roughly **87% positive reviews from around 295 Steam purchasers**.

The official fantasy is to grow a small food-processing workshop into a large facility by installing machines, automating workflows, expanding space, and managing inventory.

The core long-term fantasy is not “cut a vegetable.” It is:

> **turn manual food work into a smooth production system.**

## What it gets right

- Visible growth: small workshop → large facility.
- Machinery creates anticipation.
- Layout can become gameplay.
- Automation creates a strong “what will my operation look like later?” desire.

## The huge warning

The strongest recurring negative-review complaint is that many recipes feel like the same pipeline:

```text
CUT
→ WASH
→ DRY
→ PACKAGE
→ LABEL
→ BOX
```

again and again.

The vegetable changes and packaging may change, but the **decision structure does not**.

### Lesson

> **A new food product is not new content unless it changes what the player thinks or does.**

For *Just a Few Peppers*:

BAD:
```text
peppers → machine A
tomatoes → machine A
eggplant → machine A
```

ALSO BAD:
```text
roasted pepper jar
lyutenitsa jar
turshiya jar

same 7-step interaction
```

GOOD:
- roasted-pepper route as default;
- lyutenitsa only if it creates a real routing/material transformation decision.

If the second route does not create a meaningful choice, cut it.

## Support-tool warning

One positive-but-critical review mentions a tray meant to help carry boxes, but loading boxes onto it individually can be slower than carrying them one by one.

This is a perfect progression failure.

### Rule

Compare equal quantities through the **whole workflow**.

If a wheelbarrow carries four times as much but is awkward to load, cannot fit paths, or hits a small intake bottleneck, the upgrade failed.

## Automation warning

Do not accidentally become a shallow factory game.

*Just a Few Peppers* should have fixed authored machines, no conveyor editor, no worker AI, no production spreadsheet, no arbitrary factory layout, and no 20 recipe tiers.

Machines are **spectacle and friction removal**.

## Technical warning

Player reports and updates mention performance and employee/pathfinding problems.

Lesson:

> **Do not add workers.**

Grandpa can process a fixed batch at a fixed bench. That is state/animation, not autonomous simulation.

# 2. How to Make an Atomic Bomb in Your Garden

Steam:
https://store.steampowered.com/app/3700980/How_to_Make_an_Atomic_Bomb_in_Your_Garden/

Developer: karahan  
Release: June 9, 2026

At research time Steam showed roughly **89% positive reception from about 1,900+ reviews**.

The official game has a single absurd backyard project split into major stages and includes fundraising, buying parts, extracting needed materials from household objects, assembly, neighbor complications, scrutiny jokes, and stress-management absurdity.

## Why the premise works

The pitch is absurd before the player touches anything:

> peaceful backyard + impossible project.

The **project** creates permission for a series of strange activities.

### Biggest lesson for our title

*Just a Few Peppers* does not need to mean “roast peppers for 90 minutes.”

It can mean:

> **Grandpa said you were coming over for a few peppers; that favor expands into an entire impossible household-preparation day.**

The title becomes the setup, not a strict mechanical specification.

## What players remember

Positive reviews lean heavily into bizarre situations, dark/strange humor, neighbors, and quotable emergent moments.

Lesson:

> **Players remember situations, not crafting formulas.**

For *Just a Few Peppers*, memorable situations might be:
- wheelbarrow buried by peppers;
- dead refrigerator used as a garage cupboard;
- tarp revealing absurd machinery;
- inherited jars filling the cellar;
- returned empty jars arriving exactly when you thought you were done;
- the worktable becoming a family dinner table at the end.

## Biggest gameplay failure: low agency

Negative reviews say the game can feel like:

> buy exact object → carry it → click exact highlighted place → animation performs correct step.

Some players expected to reason about the project but found a predetermined sequence with little risk of meaningful failure.

### Lesson

Do not make *Just a Few Peppers* a highlighted checklist.

Give local freedom:
- which pile to attack;
- which path to open;
- when to collect output;
- which compatible route to use;
- whether to clear toward a visible upgrade;
- how to arrange your trips.

## Hauling warning

Negative reviews repeatedly complain about carrying many boxes into the backyard.

This is almost exactly the danger in the pepper pivot.

A funny project does **not** make hauling fun.

Every carrier upgrade should aggressively reduce trips and empty-return distance.

## QA warning

Reviews report softlocks, wheelbarrow/physics progression issues, and objects clipping/disappearing.

For our game:
- carrier contents are logical state;
- physics cascade is cosmetic;
- important tools have fixed guaranteed IDs/locations;
- equipment cannot be lost;
- objectives derive from authoritative world state;
- quitting mid-load is recoverable.

## Ending lesson

An absurd premise creates a promise of a memorable final payoff.

Do not end with a progress bar.

End with:
1. completely transformed yard;
2. visibly full cellar;
3. machinery going quiet;
4. family parcels ready;
5. Grandpa's recognition;
6. small comic gift;
7. optional final photo.

# 3. My Summer Car — cultural specificity as gameplay

Steam:
https://store.steampowered.com/app/516750/My_Summer_Car/

This game is far larger and more mechanically complex than this project should become.

Do NOT copy its scope.

The crucial lesson is that it does not merely say “this is Finland.” Its identity comes through 1990s countryside, sauna, vehicles, beer/food, dirt roads, rally culture, odd jobs, and household life.

The culture is embedded in **what the player touches**.

### Lesson for Just a Few Peppers

Do not explain Bulgarian culture through encyclopedia dialogue.

Show:
- chushkopek;
- grapevine shade;
- cellar shelves;
- inherited jars;
- returned jar boxes;
- old enamel containers;
- reused appliances;
- old tools;
- family food parcels;
- homemade labels;
- small rakia bottle near Grandpa;
- radio/yard ambience.

### What NOT to borrow

Avoid open world, survival meters, permadeath, driving, detailed mechanical assembly, errands, police, fuel, and shopping runs.

Target:

> **My Summer Car's cultural specificity compressed into one property and one afternoon.**

# 4. Barn Finders — discovery and environmental absurdity

Steam:
https://store.steampowered.com/app/991170/

Barn Finders combines searching physical spaces, finding unusual objects, humorous environments, and increasingly strange discoveries.

Players often praise exploration and finding things, but criticism points toward repetitive loops and weak progression.

### Lesson

A weird prop under every pile cannot save boring clearing.

Discoveries should be sparse, useful, and memorable.

The best discovery is:

> **an upgrade you immediately use.**

Examples:
- wheelbarrow;
- chute;
- grinder;
- cellar shortcut.

Optional family keepsakes are secondary.

# 5. Mon Bazou — mundane jobs serving a personal dream

Steam:
https://store.steampowered.com/app/1520370/

Mon Bazou combines culturally specific life, cars, money-making chores, garage progression, and multiple activities.

Its strength is that mundane jobs feed a larger personal fantasy.

Its weakness, visible even in positive reviews, is that progression can become grindy enough that chores stop feeling purposeful.

### Lesson

Every household chore in *Just a Few Peppers* should answer:

> **What visible household transformation does this create?**

If the answer is only “progress number increases,” cut it.

# 7. Revised pitch

> **Grandpa asked you to come over for “just a few peppers.” Clear an absurd late-summer Bulgarian yard, uncover generations of questionable household engineering, process the harvest, fill the winter cellar, prepare family parcels, and somehow get the place ready before you call it a day.**

The joke is “just a few peppers.”

The game is:

> **restore the household's seasonal order.**

# 8. Recommended gameplay acts

## Act 1 — Clear
Hands → crate. Learn the bulk interaction. Expose first useful equipment.

## Act 2 — Expand
Crate → wheelbarrow. Open shed/path. Start seeing cellar output.

## Act 3 — Organize
Returned jars / family parcel destinations appear. No new interaction system: carriers and fixed destinations.

## Act 4 — Transform
Optional second pepper route only if routing is actually fun. Open cellar.

## Act 5 — Victory lap
Reveal absurd homemade processor. Clear final existing supply efficiently. Finish household.

# 9. One-off household chores that add culture without new systems

These use existing handling/placement:
- return one carrier of empty jars to the correct rack;
- clear three items from Grandpa's table so it can become the final dinner table;
- clear/open the old refrigerator cabinet to reveal tools;
- move the cellar curtain once to reveal inherited jars;
- put finished family parcels by the gate;
- recover/reposition the radio so ambience changes.

These are setpieces, not management systems.

# 10. Hard rules from the new research

1. No individual pepper cooking loop after the opening joke.
2. No ten-step food pipeline.
3. No more than two functional pepper outputs in the small game.
4. A new output must change a decision.
5. Every carrier upgrade must reduce complete-workflow actions.
6. No worker AI.
7. No long empty hauling.
8. No scripted “click this exact object next” campaign.
9. No physics-critical progression objects.
10. No fake ending; yard + cellar must visibly finish.
11. Cultural identity lives in the property and objects.
12. Absurdity must create gameplay/reveals, not only dialogue.

# 11. Next test

Do NOT build the whole winter-prep version.

Prototype:

```text
ONE YARD CORNER
ONE PEPPER MOUND
ONE CRATE
ONE AUTOMATIC PROCESSOR
ONE OUTPUT CARRIER
ONE STORAGE TARGET
ONE PARTLY EXPOSED WHEELBARROW
```

Intended experience:

```text
SCOOP
→ local pile visibly shrinks

DUMP
→ machine reacts

RETURN
→ see wheel emerging

CLEAR TOWARD WHEEL
→ unlock wheelbarrow

REPEAT SAME QUANTITY
→ dramatically fewer actions

STORE FINISHED OUTPUT
→ visible shelf fills
```

If this is not satisfying, do not add winter chores.

If it works, the Bulgarian household layer can turn a good mechanic into a memorable game.
